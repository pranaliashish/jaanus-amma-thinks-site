There was once a little girl named Mira who loved pockets.

Big pockets, tiny pockets, secret pockets.

She liked to fill them with shiny pebbles, flower petals, biscuit crumbs, and little folded papers with drawings on them.

To Mira, pockets were like tiny treasure boxes.

One afternoon, while playing hide-and-seek in the house, she opened an old wooden cupboard in Mama’s room.

Inside hung Papa’s brown coat.

It had been there for a long time.

Mira had seen it before.

But today, something felt different.

She touched the coat.

It smelled like sandalwood and rain.

At the bottom was a deep pocket.

Curious, she slipped her tiny hand inside.

*Wet.*

She pulled her hand back.

*Water.*

Mira blinked.

She looked up at the window.

The sun was shining.

No rain.

She touched the pocket again.

*Still wet.*

“*That’s strange*,” she whispered.

That evening, she ran to Mama.

“*Mama! Papa’s pocket is catching rain!*”

Mama looked at her, confused.

“*Rain?*”

“*Yes! Inside the coat!*”

Mama smiled a little.

“*Maybe you imagined it.*”

But Mira knew she hadn’t.

The next morning, she checked again.

*Wet.*

The pocket still held rain.

Mira decided the pocket must be magical.

Every day, she visited it.

And every day, it was wet.

So she began leaving little things inside.

A yellow flower.

A smooth white stone.

A tiny drawing of the sun.

“*Don’t worry,*” she whispered to the pocket. “*I’ll keep you company.*”

Days passed.

The pocket became Mira’s secret friend.

Whenever she felt sad, she went to it.

Whenever she missed Papa, she touched it.

And somehow, it always felt wet.

One evening, Mama saw Mira standing by the cupboard.

“*What are you doing, little one?*”

Mira looked serious. “*Talking to the pocket.*”

Mama sat beside her. “*Why?*”

Mira hesitated. “*Because it keeps raining inside.*”

Mama gently touched the coat.

Then the pocket.

She grew very quiet.

After a long pause, Mira asked, “*Mama… how does the pocket keep rain when the sky is dry?*”

Mama took a deep breath.

And then she said softly, “*It isn’t rain.*”

Mira frowned. “*It’s not?*”

Mama shook her head.

“*When Papa felt sad, he would wipe his tears and tuck his hands into his pockets so no one would see.*”

Mira stared at the pocket. “*These are tears?*”

Mama nodded.

“***Sometimes grown-ups carry sadness quietly.***”

Mira was silent.

She had never thought of Papa crying.

Papa always looked strong.

Always smiled.

Always carried her on his shoulders.

“*But why didn’t he tell anyone?*”

Mama smiled sadly.

“***Sometimes people think they must be strong for others.***”

Mira looked at the little flower, the pebble, and the drawing she had placed inside.

“*So… the pocket was lonely?*”

Mama smiled. “*Maybe.*”

Mira gently placed her small hand into the pocket.

Not to check for rain.

But to hold the sadness.

The next morning, Mira drew a tiny note and slipped it into the pocket.

It said: **It is okay to cry**.

From then on, whenever Mira saw someone quiet, tired, or smiling too hard, she wondered:

Are they carrying rain inside?

And she became softer. Kinder. Quieter with other people’s feelings.

Because now she knew—not all rain falls from the sky.

Some rain lives in hearts.

And sometimes, love means noticing it.

## Little Reflection for Little Hearts 🌙

Sometimes people look happy on the outside but feel sad inside.

And that’s okay.

Feelings do not make us weak.

Crying does not make us small.

When someone is carrying “rain” in their heart, kindness can feel like sunshine.

And if you ever have rain inside your own heart—you can share it.

You do not have to carry it alone.
