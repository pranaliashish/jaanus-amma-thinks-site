One of the most loved teachings from the Bhagavad Gita comes from a conversation between Krishna and Prince Arjuna.

Arjuna was standing on a battlefield, feeling confused, worried, and overwhelmed.

He did not know what to do.

Like many of us, he was thinking about everything that could go wrong.

What if I fail?

What if I make a mistake?

What if things don't work out the way I hope?

Seeing Arjuna's worry, Krishna shared a powerful piece of wisdom.

He said:

**"You have a right to your actions, but not to the fruits of your actions."**

At first, that sounds like a difficult idea.

But Krishna was teaching something surprisingly simple.

He was saying:

**Focus on what you can do.**

**Do your best.**

**And do not let worry about the result stop you from trying.**

---

## 🌱 What Does This Mean for Children?

Imagine you are preparing for a school performance.

You practice your song.

You learn your lines.

You work hard.

But before the performance begins, you start worrying.

What if I forget?

What if people laugh?

What if I make a mistake?

Krishna's teaching reminds us that the only thing we can truly control is our effort.

We cannot control every outcome.

We cannot control what other people think.

We cannot control every surprise that may happen.

But we can choose to prepare well and give our best.

That is enough.

---

## 🪁 A Simple Example

Imagine flying a kite.

You can hold the string carefully.

You can run with patience.

You can learn how the wind works.

But you cannot control every gust of wind in the sky.

Some things are in your hands.

Some things are not.

Wisdom is knowing the difference.

---

## 📖 The Lesson from the Gita

Krishna was not telling Arjuna to stop caring.

He was teaching him not to become trapped by worry.

When we focus only on results, we often become afraid.

When we focus on effort, we become brave enough to begin.

Whether it is learning something new, making a friend, helping at home, or trying our best at school, the same wisdom applies.

Give your best.

Learn from the experience.

And let go of the rest.

---

## 🌿 Why This Wisdom Still Matters Today

Children today often feel pressure to be the best.

Parents sometimes feel pressure too.

But the Bhagavad Gita gently reminds us that our worth is not measured by trophies, marks, or perfect outcomes.

What matters is sincerity.

What matters is effort.

What matters is showing up with a willing heart.

Success may come.

Mistakes may happen.

But every honest effort helps us grow.

And growth is a beautiful result in itself.
