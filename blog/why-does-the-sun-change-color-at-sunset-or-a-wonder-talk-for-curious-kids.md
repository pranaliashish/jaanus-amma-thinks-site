A Jaanu–Amma Wonder Talk

**Jaanu:** Amma, why does the Sun turn orange at sunset?

**Amma:** That's a wonderful question.

What color is the Sun during the middle of the day?

**Jaanu:** Yellow!

**Amma:** It often looks yellow to us.

But would you believe me if I told you the Sun's light actually contains many colors?

**Jaanu:** Like a rainbow?

**Amma:** Exactly!

Sunlight is made up of all the colors of the rainbow mixed together.

**Jaanu:** Then why don't we see all the colors?

**Amma:** Because during the day, the sunlight reaches us almost directly.

But at sunset, something different happens.

**Jaanu:** What happens?

**Amma:** The Sun sits much lower in the sky.

Its light has to travel through a lot more air before it reaches our eyes.

**Jaanu:** Does the air change the colors?

**Amma:** It does.

The blue and violet colors get scattered in different directions.

That leaves more of the red, orange, and yellow colors for us to see.

**Jaanu:** So that's why the sky looks orange?

**Amma:** Yes!

The sunlight is showing us a different part of its rainbow.

**Jaanu:** It's like the sky is painting a picture!

**Amma:** That's a beautiful way to think about it.

Every sunset is a little masterpiece made from light and air.

🌅🧡

## ✨ Tiny Wonder

Sunsets don't look the same everywhere.

Dust, clouds, water droplets, and even tiny particles in the air can change the colors we see.

That's why some sunsets are golden, some are pink, and some are deep orange or red.

---

## 🔬 Science Behind It

Sunlight contains all the colors of the rainbow.

When sunlight travels through Earth's atmosphere, tiny particles in the air scatter the shorter blue and violet wavelengths more easily.

During sunset, sunlight has to travel through much more atmosphere than it does during the middle of the day.

By the time the light reaches our eyes, much of the blue light has been scattered away, allowing the reds, oranges, and yellows to become more visible.

That's what gives sunsets their beautiful warm colors.
