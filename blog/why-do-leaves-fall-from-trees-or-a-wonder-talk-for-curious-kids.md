A Jaanu-Amma wonder talk ✨

**Jaanu:** Amma, why do leaves fall from trees?

**Amma:** That's a lovely question.

Have you ever noticed that some trees stay green all year, while others drop all their leaves?

**Jaanu:** Yes! In autumn, the ground is covered with leaves.

**Amma:** It almost looks like the trees are making a colorful blanket.

**Jaanu:** But why do they let their leaves fall?

**Amma:** Imagine wearing a thick winter coat on a very hot day.

Wouldn't it feel uncomfortable?

**Jaanu:** It would!

**Amma:** Trees have their own way of getting ready for different seasons.

When colder weather is coming, some trees stop making food in their leaves.

Instead of spending energy keeping every leaf alive, they save that energy for the winter.

**Jaanu:** So the tree lets the leaves go?

**Amma:** Yes.

Before the leaves fall, the tree gently closes the tiny place where each leaf is attached to the branch.

Then, when the wind blows, the leaf floats down to the ground.

**Jaanu:** Does the tree get sad?

**Amma:** I like to think the tree knows something important.

It knows that letting go isn't the end.

When spring comes, new leaves will grow.

**Jaanu:** So the tree isn't losing everything?

**Amma:** No.

It's simply resting and getting ready for a new beginning.

🍂🤎

## ✨ Tiny Wonder

A single large tree can grow **thousands of leaves** in one season.

Even after losing them all, it can grow a brand-new canopy when spring arrives.

Nature reminds us that endings often make room for new beginnings.

---

## 🔬 Science Behind It

Leaves help trees make food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide through a process called **photosynthesis**.

As the days become shorter and colder, some trees prepare for winter by slowing down.

They stop sending food and water to the leaves and form a special layer where each leaf joins the branch.

Eventually, the leaf detaches and falls.

This helps the tree save water and energy until warmer weather returns.

When spring arrives, tiny new buds open into fresh green leaves.

## 🌙 Try It Tonight

The next time you're outside together, pick up a fallen leaf.

Look closely.

Notice its colors.

Its tiny lines.

Its shape.

Then ask your little one:

💭 *"What do you think this tree is getting ready for?"*

Sometimes nature teaches us that letting go is not about losing something.

Sometimes it's about making space for something new.
