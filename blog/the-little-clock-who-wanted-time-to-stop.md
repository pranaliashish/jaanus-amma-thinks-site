In the corner of a warm little house, on a pale yellow wall, hung a clock named Tock.

Tock was old.

Not very shiny.

Not very grand.

But he was faithful.

*Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

Every morning, he woke the house gently.

Every afternoon, he counted the hours of laughter.

And every night, he listened to bedtime whispers.

Tock *loved* his house.

He knew its sounds.

The soft footsteps of Mom in the kitchen.

The little running feet of a child.

The deep laughter of Dad.

And every evening, just before dinner, Dad would look up at Tock and smile.

“*Right on time,*” he would say.

Tock *loved* that.

It made him tick a little prouder.

*Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

But one morning, something felt different.

The house was quiet.

*Too* quiet.

No laughter. No footsteps. No warm humming from the kitchen.

Just silence.

Tock waited.

*Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

The door opened.

Mom came in.

Her eyes were red.

The little child held her hand tightly.

No one spoke.

That evening, Dad did not come home.

Tock kept watching the door.

Waiting. *Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

But Dad did not come.

The next day, many people came.

They spoke softly.

Some cried. Some hugged Mom.

The little child asked, “*When will Dad come back?*”

No one answered.

Tock felt something heavy in the room.

A sadness so deep it made even the walls feel still.

But Tock kept ticking.

*Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

And suddenly, Tock hated it.

His sound felt wrong.

Too loud. Too normal.

How could time keep moving normally when the house had changed?

How could minutes pass when someone was missing?

That night, Tock whispered to the Moon through the window, “*I want to stop.*”

The Moon listened. “*Why?*” she asked.

“*If I stop,*” said Tock, “*maybe everything will stay here. Maybe nothing else will change.*”

The Moon was quiet for a while.

Then she asked, “*And if you stop… will he return?*”

Tock had no answer.

---



Days passed.

The little child cried less.

But the house still felt empty.

Tock watched Mom sit quietly by the window.

He wished his hands would freeze.

He wished mornings would not come.

Because every new day felt like leaving Dad further behind.

One rainy afternoon, the little child stood beneath Tock and asked Mom,

“*Will I forget Dad’s voice?*”

Mom knelt and held the child close.

And she said something Tock would never forget.

“*No, dear. Time does not take away love. It teaches us how to carry it.*”

Tock fell silent inside.

That night, for the first time, he listened to his own ticking.

*Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

It no longer sounded cruel.

It sounded gentle.

Like breathing.

Like healing.

Like carrying.

---



Weeks passed.

The house slowly changed.

Not back to the way it was.

But into something softer.

Mom laughed again. *Sometimes*.

The little child smiled more. *Sometimes*.

And every evening, before bed, the child looked up at Tock and whispered, “*Goodnight*.”

Just like Dad used to.

And Tock understood.

Time was not stealing the past.

It was stitching it into memory. Keeping it safe. Carrying love forward.

And for the *first* time—Tock did not want to stop.

*Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

Because moving forward was not leaving love behind.

It was learning how to walk with it.

And every night, under the quiet moonlight, Tock kept time.

Not because time heals everything.

But because time helps hearts heal enough to keep loving.

## *Gentle Goodnight Reflection 🌙*

Sometimes life changes in ways we do not choose.

And when it hurts, we may wish time would stop.

But time does something quiet and kind.

It carries us.

Not away from love—but forward with it.

The people we love may leave the room, but never the heart.

Goodnight, little heart. Love stays. Always. 🤍

### **For Parents:**

This story gently introduces the idea that love remains with us even when someone we love is no longer physically present. 

It can help start age-appropriate conversations about grief, remembrance, and healing.
