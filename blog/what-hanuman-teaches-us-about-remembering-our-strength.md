When people think of Hanuman, they often think of extraordinary strength.

The mighty leap across the ocean.

The mountain carried through the sky.

The fearless devotion to Lord Rama.

But one of the most beautiful lessons from the Ramayana comes from a surprising fact:

There was a time when Hanuman forgot how powerful he was.

---



## The Moment Before the Great Leap

When Sita was taken to Lanka, Rama's allies searched far and wide to find her.

Eventually, they reached the edge of a vast ocean.

Across the water lay Lanka.

The problem was obvious.

Someone would have to leap across the ocean to reach it.

The vanaras gathered together and discussed who might be able to make such a journey.

One by one, they spoke about their abilities.

Some could jump a little distance.

Some could jump farther.

But none believed they could cross the entire ocean.

Hanuman sat quietly among them.

He listened.

He doubted.

He did not volunteer.

Not because he lacked strength.

But because he had forgotten it.

---



## Jambavan's Reminder

At that moment, the wise elder Jambavan turned to Hanuman.

He reminded him of who he truly was.

He spoke of Hanuman's courage.

His gifts.

His abilities.

The strength that had always been within him.

As Hanuman listened, something changed.

Little by little, he remembered.

The strength had never disappeared.

It had simply been forgotten.

And once he remembered, he rose to his feet, grew in confidence, and prepared for the leap that would become one of the most famous moments in the Ramayana.

---



## A Lesson for Modern Life

Many of us think strength is something we must gain.

But sometimes strength is something we already possess.

We simply lose sight of it.

A difficult season.

A mistake.

A disappointment.

A failure.

These experiences can make us forget what we are capable of.

Like Hanuman, we begin to underestimate ourselves.

---



## A Lesson for Parents and Children

One of the greatest gifts we can offer a child is encouragement.

Not empty praise.

But gentle reminders of who they are.

Sometimes children need someone to say:

"I've seen your kindness."

"I've seen your courage."

"I know you can try again."

Those words can become a modern version of Jambavan's reminder.

And sometimes adults need those reminders too.

---



## Ancient Wisdom for Modern Life

The story of Hanuman reminds us that confidence does not always come from becoming stronger.

Sometimes it comes from remembering the strength that was there all along.

The leap across the ocean began long before Hanuman left the ground.

It began the moment he remembered who he was.
