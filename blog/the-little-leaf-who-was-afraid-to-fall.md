In the middle of a tall green tree lived a little leaf.

She was soft, bright, and full of life.

Every morning she danced with the wind.

Every afternoon she played in the sunshine.

And every night she slept under the quiet song of crickets.

Life felt safe.

Life felt familiar.

Until one day—the tree began to change.

Some leaves around her turned yellow.

Some turned orange.

And one by one—they began to fall.

The little leaf trembled.

“*What is happening?*” she whispered.

An older leaf beside her smiled gently.

“*It is time.*”

“*Time for what?*” asked the little leaf.

“*Time to let go.*”

The little leaf held tighter to her branch.

“*No! I don’t want to fall. What if I get lost? What if I get hurt? What if I never come back?*”

The old leaf swayed softly.

“*I felt that too, once.*”

“*But weren’t you scared?*” asked the little leaf.

“*Yes,*” said the old leaf.

“**But change is not always something to fear.**”

The little leaf looked down.

The ground seemed so far away.

“*What if the ground is cold?*”

The old leaf laughed kindly.

“*The earth knows how to hold us.*”

That night, the wind grew stronger.

A few more leaves floated down.

They did not crash.

They danced. They spun. They glided.

The little leaf watched.

“*They look happy,*” she said.

“*They trusted the wind,*” said the old leaf.

Days passed.

The little leaf noticed the tree growing quieter. Emptier. Different.

And though she was still afraid—she began to understand.

Not everything stays the same forever.

And maybe that was alright.

Then one morning, the wind whispered her name.

It was time.

The little leaf shook.

“*I’m scared.*”

The tree spoke for the first time.

“*My little one,*” it said gently,

“*You are not falling away from me. You are becoming something new.*”

The little leaf took a deep breath.

And let go.

The wind caught her. Softly.

She spun through the golden air.

Dancing. Floating. Laughing.

And when she reached the earth—it was warm. Soft. Welcoming.

Just as the old leaf had said.

The little leaf rested there peacefully.

And for the first time, she understood:

**Letting go was not the end.**

**Sometimes it was the beginning of becoming something new.**

And high above, the tree stood proudly.

Waiting for spring.

## *Gentle takeaway for parents:*

Children often fear change—new places, new people, new routines.

This story helps them understand:

*Change can feel scary, but it can also carry us gently into something beautiful.*
