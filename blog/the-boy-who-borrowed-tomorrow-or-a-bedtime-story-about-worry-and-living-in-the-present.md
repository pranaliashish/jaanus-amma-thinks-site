There was once a little boy named Avi who worried about everything.

He worried before bedtime.

He worried before school.

He worried before rain.

He even worried when the sun was shining.

“What if tomorrow is hard?” he would ask.

“What if I forget my lesson?”

“What if no one plays with me?”

“What if something goes wrong?”

Tomorrow was Avi’s favorite place to visit.

Even when he was living inside today.

One evening, while staring at the moon from his window, Avi heard a strange knock.

*Tap. Tap. Tap*.

At his windowsill sat a tiny old man.

No taller than a teapot.

He wore a silver coat and carried a bag full of glowing glass jars.

“*Who are you?*” Avi whispered.

The old man bowed. “*I am Tomorrow Keeper.*”

“*Tomorrow Keeper?*”

The little man nodded. “*I keep all the tomorrows.*”

Avi’s eyes widened. “*All of them?*”

“*All of them.*”

Inside the bag, tiny glowing jars shimmered like stars.

Each one held a tomorrow.

A school tomorrow.

A rainy tomorrow.

A birthday tomorrow.

A hard tomorrow.

A happy tomorrow.

Avi leaned closer. “*Can I see mine?*”

Tomorrow Keeper smiled. “*You may borrow one.*”

Avi carefully took a jar.

Inside, he saw himself walking into school.

But his paper slipped from his hand.

Everyone laughed.

Avi gasped. “*Oh no.*”

That night, he could not sleep.

The whole time he worried about the tomorrow inside the jar.

The next day came.

But it wasn’t the same.

His paper did not fall.

No one laughed.

Everything was fine.

That evening, Tomorrow Keeper returned. “*You looked.*”

Avi nodded. “*And it was wrong.*”

Tomorrow Keeper smiled.

“*Tomorrows are only guesses.*”

But Avi had an idea.

“*If I can borrow tomorrow,*” he thought, “*I can prepare.*”

So every night, he borrowed another one.

A rainy tomorrow.

A hard test tomorrow.

A lonely tomorrow.

A sick-day tomorrow.

And every night he worried.

He packed extra pencils. Extra socks. Extra snacks. Extra plans.

Soon, Avi’s room was full of tomorrows.

Glowing jars stacked everywhere.

And Avi was exhausted.

He stopped playing. Stopped laughing. Stopped climbing trees.

Because he was always preparing. Always worrying. Always borrowing.

One afternoon, his mother found him sitting among jars.

“*Avi,*” she said gently, “*why aren’t you outside?*”

Avi looked tired. “*I’m getting ready.*”

“*For what?*”

He pointed. “*For tomorrow.*”

Mama looked at the glowing jars.

And then at Avi. “*But what about today?*”

Avi frowned. “*Today?*”

Mama sat beside him. “*Yes.*”

“*The butterflies are outside today.*”

“*The mango tree is waiting today.*”

“*The rain smells sweet today.*”

“*But what if tomorrow is bad?*” Avi asked.

Mama smiled. “*What if tomorrow is beautiful?*”

Avi looked at the jars.

He had never thought of that.

That night, Tomorrow Keeper came again.

His bag was much lighter now.

“*You have borrowed too many tomorrows*,” he said.

Avi looked down. “*I just wanted to be ready.*”

Tomorrow Keeper sat beside him.

“*Tell me—did borrowing tomorrow make today better?*”

Avi thought. *No*.

It made today smaller.

Smaller and tighter.

Like a shirt he could not breathe in.

Tomorrow Keeper held out his hand. “*Would you like to return them?*”

Avi nodded.

One by one, he returned the jars.

The rainy tomorrow.

The hard tomorrow.

The lonely tomorrow.

The scary tomorrow.

With each jar, the room felt lighter.

And so did Avi.

When the last jar was gone, Tomorrow Keeper smiled.

“***Tomorrow will come by itself***,” he said. “***You do not have to carry it before it arrives***.”

That night, Avi slept deeply.

The next morning, he ran outside barefoot.

The sun was warm.

The butterflies were waiting.

The mango tree swayed.

And for the first time in a long time, Avi was not visiting tomorrow.

He was here.

In today.

And today, was enough.

## Little Reflection for Little Hearts 🌙

Sometimes we worry about tomorrow so much that we forget today.

But tomorrow is not here yet.

And many of the scary things we imagine never happen at all.

When worry comes, take a breath and look around.

What is here right now?

The sky. The trees. Your people. Your little joys.

Today is real.

Tomorrow can wait.
