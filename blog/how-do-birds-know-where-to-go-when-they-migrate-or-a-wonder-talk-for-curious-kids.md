A Jaanu–Amma Wonder Talk

**Jaanu:** Amma, how do birds know where to go when they migrate?

**Amma:** That's a wonderful question.

Have you seen birds flying together in the sky?

**Jaanu:** Yes! Sometimes they fly very far away.

**Amma:** They do.

Some birds travel thousands of kilometers every year.

**Jaanu:** But how do they know which way to go?

**Amma:** Imagine if someone dropped you in a new place without a map.

Would it be easy to find your way home?

**Jaanu:** No!

**Amma:** Yet many birds do exactly that.

They travel across countries, oceans, mountains, and forests—and still find the places they need to go.

**Jaanu:** Do they have maps?

**Amma:** Not the kind we use.

Scientists believe birds use many clues to help them navigate.

**Jaanu:** What kind of clues?

**Amma:** Some birds use the position of the Sun.

Some use the stars.

Some remember rivers, coastlines, and mountains.

And some can even sense Earth's magnetic field.

**Jaanu:** Earth's magnetic field?

**Amma:** Yes!

The Earth acts a little bit like a giant magnet.

Scientists think some birds can feel this invisible force and use it like a natural compass.

**Jaanu:** So they carry a compass inside them?

**Amma:** In a way, yes.

Nature gave them one.

**Jaanu:** That's amazing!

**Amma:** It really is.

Every year, tiny birds set off on journeys longer than many humans will ever travel.

And somehow, they find their way.

🐦🤍

## ✨ Tiny Wonder

The Arctic Tern travels farther than almost any other animal on Earth.

During its yearly migration, it can travel around **70,000 kilometers** as it journeys between the Arctic and Antarctica.

That's almost enough to go around the Earth twice!

---

## 🔬 Science Behind It

Scientists are still learning exactly how birds navigate.

But research suggests that many birds use several tools at once:

- The position of the Sun during the day
- The stars at night
- Familiar landmarks such as rivers and coastlines
- Earth's magnetic field

Using all these clues together helps birds travel incredible distances and still find their way.

It's like having a compass, a map, and a memory book all at the same time.

## 🌙 Try It Tonight

The next time you go outside with your little one, look up at the sky.

If you see birds flying overhead, ask:

💭 *"How do you think they know where they're going?"*

Then imagine setting off on a journey without a phone, map, or GPS.

Nature has given birds remarkable ways to find their path.

And every time we watch them fly, we're witnessing one of the world's greatest adventures.
